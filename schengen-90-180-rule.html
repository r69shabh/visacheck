The Schengen 90/180 rule explained

The standard short-stay limit is 90 days in any rolling 180-day period. For each day you plan to be in Schengen, count that date and the 179 calendar days before it. No more than 90 of those dates can be days you were in the Schengen Area on a short stay.

What goes into the count?

Count calendar dates, not hotel nights: a Friday arrival and Sunday departure uses three days. Add dates across Schengen countries, counting a day only once even if you cross an internal border. Periods authorized under a residence permit or long-stay visa do not count toward the short-stay allowance; if you’re traveling outside the country that issued yours, check which rules apply to that trip.

The 90-day limit is a ceiling, not permission to enter. If you have a short-stay visa, also check its validity dates, number of entries and authorized duration. Your sticker may allow fewer days.

Example 1: Several visits in the same window

In 2026, you stay 30 days from 1–30 March and 31 days from 1–31 May. You plan to return from 1–30 July. On 30 July, your 180-day window starts on 1 February; all three visits are inside it. The total is 30 + 31 + 30 = 91 days. A 29 July departure instead would make the total 90, assuming no other stays or visa restrictions.

Example 2: Old days fall out one at a time

Assume your only visit was 1 January–31 March 2026 (90 days). On 29 June, the window still includes 1 January, so returning that day would bring the total to 91. On 30 June, the window starts on 2 January: 89 earlier days plus the new arrival day makes 90. You have room that day because 1 January fell out, not because the allowance reset at the border.
