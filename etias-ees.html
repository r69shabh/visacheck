VisaCheck / ETIAS and EES

ETIAS vs EES: what changes for travelers?

EES records border crossings. ETIAS is a pre-travel authorization. They are separate EU systems, and neither changes the standard 90 days in any 180 days short-stay limit.

EES: registration at the border

The Entry/Exit System records entries, exits and refusals at participating countries’ external borders for covered non-EU short-stay travelers, whether or not they need a short-stay visa. It records travel-document data and may collect a facial image and fingerprints. It is not a visa or a travel authorization you buy before departure.

The rollout began on 12 October 2025 and the Commission says EES became fully operational on 10 April 2026. See the Commission’s EES page for scope and exceptions, including those relevant to permit holders.

ETIAS: authorization before travel when required

ETIAS stands for European Travel Information and Authorisation System. When it applies, eligible visa-exempt travelers will need this authorization before short trips to participating European countries. It is not a Schengen visa or a substitute for one if your passport requires a visa.

The Commission says it will announce the exact start date in advance. Check the Commission’s ETIAS launch notice for the latest timing and instructions. Don’t submit passport details or pay a site claiming to issue ETIAS before official applications are open. Once open, use links from the official EU travel site.

What should you do before you leave?

  1. Check whether your passport needs a visa for your destinations. If it does, follow the relevant consulate’s application instructions; ETIAS does not replace them.
  2. If you’re visa-exempt, check whether ETIAS applies on your arrival date and whether any exception covers you. Expect EES registration if you’re in its scope.
  3. Keep a record of your actual entry and exit dates and check your planned stay against the short-stay limit.
