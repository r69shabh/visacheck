Schengen visa refusal reasons: what the notice means

The refusal notice should say why your short-stay visa was refused. Start with every box marked on your own notice and any additional remarks. The numbers below refer to the standard EU form (Annex VI); use the wording on your notice if its numbering differs.

Read the marked grounds and check the corresponding evidence

Box 2: Purpose and conditions of stay not justified
Show why you’re going and how the trip works: an itinerary covering the whole stay, accommodation or host evidence and, if relevant, a conference registration or family invitation in the required format.
Box 10: Purpose and conditions not considered reliable
Compare dates, destinations, hosts and traveller names across your form, transport, accommodation and invitation. Explain genuine discrepancies and replace outdated or unverifiable reservations with current evidence.
Boxes 3–4: Insufficient proof of funds or of lawful access to them
Check the destination’s current requirements for the amount, statement period and format. Show who will pay for accommodation, daily costs and the return journey. If sponsored, use the official sponsorship evidence and show the sponsor’s financial means as required. Explain unusual deposits with genuine records.
Box 13: Doubts about leaving before the visa expires
Show why you’ll return, for example with approved leave and a return-to-work date, an enrolment letter and term dates, or other relevant obligations in your country of residence. A return reservation alone may not answer this concern.
Boxes 11–12: Doubts about statements or supporting documents
Read the remarks to see what isn’t credible. Correct inaccurate information, obtain current records from their original issuers and include required translations. A false, counterfeit or forged travel document is a separate ground (box 1); never submit altered evidence.
Box 16: Proof of adequate and valid medical insurance not provided
Ask for a certificate showing the insured traveller, territorial coverage, travel dates and required medical/repatriation benefits. See the €30,000 insurance checklist.
Box 5: 90 days already spent in the current 180-day period
Recount actual entry and exit dates and check proposed new dates against the rolling window. Use the 90/180 calculator guide.

Should I appeal or reapply?

Under Article 32, you can appeal against the member state that made the final decision, under that state’s national law. Use the notice to find the authority, deadline and procedure; don’t assume the application centre handles appeals or that reapplying stops an appeal deadline. If you decide to reapply, address each ground with stronger or corrected evidence rather than resubmitting the same file. For an entry alert or a public-policy, security or health ground, consult the authority named on the notice or seek qualified advice.

Sources: EU Visa Code, Article 32 and Annex VI, the standard notification form. This is general information, not legal advice. Follow your refusal notice and confirm the current procedure with the deciding authority.
