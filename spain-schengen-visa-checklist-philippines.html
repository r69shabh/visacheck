Spain Schengen visa document checklist from the Philippines

Use this checklist if you live in the Philippines, need a short-stay tourist visa and Spain is your main destination. The Spanish Consulate General in Manila lists proof of residence in its consular district as a separate required item.

Review your trip and documents in the free VisaCheck checker.

Use the Manila consulate’s list to assemble your file

  1. Application and photo: Complete and sign the official form for each applicant and bring a recent colour passport-style photo on a light background. Check the separate signature and filing instructions if applying for a child.
  2. Passport and copies: Bring the original and a copy of the biometric data pages. The passport must have two blank pages, have been issued within the past ten years and be valid at least three months beyond your planned departure from Schengen.
  3. Residence: Include proof you live in the consular district. If you aren’t a Philippine citizen, check which document the mission accepts as proof of legal residence.
  4. Trip and return evidence: Prepare an itinerary, accommodation evidence for each stop, and transport reservations in line with the current tourism list. Check the host document required if staying with friends or family. Use work, study or other evidence relevant to your return; the consulate asks for proof of intent to leave as well as proof of funds.
  5. Finances: Verify the current Spanish amount and what records the local checklist accepts. If you’re relying on a sponsor, make it clear who pays and check the required sponsorship documents.
  6. Insurance: Bring proof of at least €30,000 travel medical coverage. The Manila guide specifies all of Schengen, your whole stay and cover for urgent care, emergency hospital treatment and repatriation for medical reasons or death.

Before you travel to the appointment: Start from the Spanish Consulate in Manila’s Schengen visa page for where and how to submit. Check the latest tourism instructions for originals, copies and supporting evidence; the consulate may ask for more documents or an interview.

Sources: Spanish Consulate General in Manila and EU Visa Code, Article 14. The official consulate and its current tourism checklist take precedence over this summary.
