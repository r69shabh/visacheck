Italy Schengen visa document checklist from Saudi Arabia

Use this checklist if you legally live in Saudi Arabia, need a short-stay tourist visa and Italy is your main destination. Italy’s embassy names Riyadh, Jeddah and Dammam as application-centre locations; confirm which one handles your application before booking.

Review your trip and documents in the free VisaCheck checker.

Gather the evidence for a tourist application

  1. Get the current tourism list. Start at the Italian Embassy in Riyadh’s visa page and follow the application centre it currently links for documents and appointments. Use Visa for Italy to check the general requirements for your nationality, residence and purpose.
  2. Identify yourself and where you live. Prepare your signed form, passport and photo. If you are not a Saudi citizen, have your Saudi residence permit (iqama) ready and confirm what the local checklist requires for copies, validity and proof you can return.
  3. Explain the whole trip. Assemble the itinerary, entry and return reservations, and hotel or private-host evidence. If spending some nights outside Italy, show those stays too; the documents should make clear why Italy is the main destination.
  4. Show funds and circumstances. Check which bank records and income, study or other evidence the tourism list asks for. If sponsored, check what documents the sponsor must supply and whether a specific declaration is required. Check the mission’s translation instructions for non-Italian documents rather than assuming a translation is or isn’t needed.
  5. Bring the policy certificate. Check the dates, traveller names, medical and repatriation benefits and €30,000 minimum.

Before the appointment: Compare each original and copy against the current tourism list from the embassy’s linked centre. Don’t reuse another country’s document list or an old appointment link.

Sources: Italian Embassy in Riyadh, Italy’s foreign ministry visa portal and EU Visa Code, Article 14. Confirm current requirements and filing instructions with the official mission.
