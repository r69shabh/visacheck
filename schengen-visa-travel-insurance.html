Schengen visa travel insurance: the €30,000 rule

For a short-stay Schengen visa, travel medical insurance generally needs at least €30,000 of coverage, valid throughout the territories where the visa applies and for every day of the intended stay, including your exit day. The €30,000 is the coverage limit, not the price of the policy.

What to check on the certificate and in the policy

  • Who’s insured: Look for each applicant’s name as it appears on their passport. If coverage comes with a credit card or group policy, ask for a certificate that identifies the covered travellers and confirms any activation conditions are met.
  • Where: A uniform visa generally calls for coverage across Schengen, not just the issuing country. Article 15 has a separate territorial rule for a limited-territorial-validity visa covering more than one member state.
  • When: A trip entering France on 3 May and leaving Spain on 14 May needs coverage on both dates and every intervening date. If the policy expires on 13 May, ask for an updated certificate before applying.
  • What: Check for urgent medical attention, emergency hospital treatment, medical repatriation and costs in case of death. An accident-only or trip-cancellation policy isn’t proof of all these benefits.
  • How much: If the policy is in USD or another currency, ask the insurer to confirm the euro equivalent and the mission whether it accepts that evidence. Don’t assume 30,000 units of another currency equals €30,000.

Do I need insurance for every trip on a multiple-entry visa?

For a multiple-entry application, Article 15 requires proof covering the first intended visit. You also acknowledge the need for insurance on later stays. For a one- or two-entry application, show coverage for the intended stay or stays. Check the mission’s instructions for acceptable certificates or insurers and how it handles any applicable exemption.

Sources: EU Visa Code, Article 15 and the European Commission’s application guide. Confirm the exact evidence with the official consulate; a policy passing the checker is not a guarantee of acceptance.
